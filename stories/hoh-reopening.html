Back to Hoh Rainforest

Trip highlight · Olympic National Park · May 8, 2025

The day the Hoh reopened.

It’s easy to take an open park for granted until the road that brings you there is closed. After driving from Florida, being there for the reopening of the road to the Hoh Rainforest made me think about what access to these places really means. A forest can exist for centuries, but the chance to stand beneath its trees can change the way someone sees the world. I felt grateful to be there, to hear Governor Ferguson speak, and to see people come together to make that experience possible again.

A day I’ll remember

On May 8, 2025, the road to the Hoh Rainforest reopened after months of closure from storm damage. Standing there and hearing Governor Bob Ferguson speak, I kept thinking about how much it matters that people can get into these parks. We had driven across the country for places like this. Being there when access returned became one of the moments that stayed with me.

We got to meet Governor Ferguson after his speech and take a photo with him. I was also interviewed by KIRO7 about our visit. I’m thankful for both opportunities, but what made the day special was being there with people who understood how much this place matters.

“Inspiring — the way they all spoke about this. You could feel it.”

— My words in the KIRO7 report

Walking the trail together

After the ceremony, we got to hike with Governor Ferguson. I’m grateful we had the chance to share that part of the day with him. Hearing about the reopening was meaningful, but walking into the forest afterward made it feel real. The road had opened, and now the trail was carrying us into the very place everyone had worked to make accessible again.

For me, that walk brought everything together. Keeping a park open means leaving a door open for someone to discover something bigger than themselves. The ribbon we carried away reminds me of that doorway, and of how fortunate we were to walk through it that day.

A ribbon that stayed with us

After the ceremony, we were given a piece of the ribbon. We kept it in our car as a good luck charm for the rest of the trip, and now it’s framed in my house. To me, it represents more than that day. It reminds me that the places we love depend on people caring for them, and that keeping them open gives others the chance to make memories of their own.
